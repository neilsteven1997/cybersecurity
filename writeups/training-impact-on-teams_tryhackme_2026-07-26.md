# Training Impact on Teams

---

Mastering any skill demands consistent practice and ongoing refinement, and cyber security follows the same principle. No one enters 
the field already proficient; the necessary abilities must be acquired and then continually sharpened. Hands-on practice, however, 
carries practical constraints. It typically requires a dedicated computer laboratory isolated from production systems so that 
experimentation cannot introduce risk. Dedicated training platforms therefore become essential for anyone aiming to reach genuine 
expertise.

The familiar adage that an ounce of prevention outweighs a pound of cure applies directly. When responsibility for system security 
rests with a team, it is far preferable to develop competence inside a controlled training environment rather than under the pressure
of a live incident. Well-structured training simultaneously raises the overall quality of the team’s output and thereby lowers the 
probability that incidents will occur in the first place.

From an organisational standpoint the most compelling advantage is the ability to expand capacity without recruiting additional staff.
Prepared personnel already possess the concepts, techniques and tools required when an attack materialises, rather than having to
acquire them under fire. Training also makes it practical to bring junior colleagues on board and accelerate their ramp-up, while 
relieving senior staff of the repetitive burden of teaching the same material. Centralised programmes further establish a shared 
baseline against which skills and progress can be measured with precision, replacing vague labels such as “junior” or “senior” with
concrete descriptions that support better task allocation and clearer career pathways. In addition, collaborative exercises such as 
Capture the Flag challenges can strengthen interpersonal bonds that later prove valuable under operational stress.

For smaller groups, ready-made training content is ordinarily sufficient. Once headcount exceeds roughly twenty people, or when the 
organisation’s requirements become highly specialised, customisation becomes advantageous. TryHackMe supports this through its Content
Studio, which permits existing modules to be adapted or entirely new ones created around the organisation’s particular priorities. 
Large enterprises also expect seamless integration with their existing technology landscape; support for Single Sign-On and 
well-documented application programming interfaces is therefore a practical necessity.

Companies that treat employee development as an investment routinely see measurable returns. Consider a ten-person cyber security team 
in which each member costs eighty thousand dollars annually. If training lifts productivity by four percent, the resulting annual gain
equals thirty-two thousand dollars. At a training cost of five hundred dollars per person the total outlay is five thousand dollars,
producing a Return On Investment of six hundred and forty percent. Experience at TryHackMe indicates that a concise proposal framed 
around these figures markedly improves the chance of securing budget approval; the attached template titled “Cost savings by investing
in Cyber Security training” has proved effective in practice.

Selecting the right vendor requires deliberate evaluation of several factors: the precise audience and their experience levels, roles
and relevant topics; the vendor’s track record with comparable organisations; the breadth, depth and quality of content aligned with 
organisational priorities; the ability for users to learn, practise and train within a single platform; and the recognition that,
relative to the cost of cyber security talent itself, training expenditure is almost always modest.

Training remains an indispensable component of any high-performing cyber security function. Knowledge workers in particular tend to 
embrace lifelong learning, and organisations that encourage or mandate regular development find the practice beneficial for both 
individuals and the enterprise. Further details on accelerating organisational learning are available at business.tryhackme.com, while
educational institutions can explore dedicated offerings under TryHackMe Classrooms.
